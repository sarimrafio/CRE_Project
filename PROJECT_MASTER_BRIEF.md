# MASTER PROJECT BRIEF
## Comparative Dynamic Modelling, Design, and Sustainability Optimization of CSTR and PFR Systems for Biological Treatment of Textile Wastewater: A Literature-Calibrated Digital Twin Approach

- **Project Number (Course Guideline):** Project 24: Comparative CSTR and PFR Design for Biological Wastewater Treatment
- **Application Area:** Biological treatment of textile wastewater
- **Biological Kinetic Model:** Advanced Monod kinetics with biomass balance and oxygen limitation
- **Industrial Scope:** Textile wastewater only
- **Primary Comparison:** Ideal continuous stirred-tank reactor (CSTR) vs ideal plug-flow reactor (PFR)
- **Work Allocation:** 4-member research team structure (Member 1: Literature & Evidence; Member 2: Reaction Engineering; Member 3: Scientific Computing & Sustainability; Member 4: Digital Twin & Visualization)
- **Environment:** Windows 11, Python scientific stack (NumPy, SciPy, Pandas, Matplotlib, Plotly) + Antigravity Orchestration with OpenCode Free-Tier / Gemini Paid routing

---

### Core Course Methodology (from `Project Guideline.pdf`)
1. **Step 1 — Literature Data Collection**: Published reaction-rate expressions, kinetic parameters ($\mu_{\max}, K_S, K_O, Y, b, k_La$), operating conditions, conversion data, energy requirements.
2. **Step 2 — Reaction Engineering Calculations**: Mole balances, rate equations, design equations (Fogler's methodology), conversion, residence time, reactor volume.
3. **Step 3 — Simulation and Optimization**: Python-based numerical integration, CSTR and PFR comparative simulation, parameter sensitivity, multi-objective design optimization.
4. **Step 4 — Sustainability Assessment**: Energy consumption (aeration, pumping, mixing), operational costs, electricity-related GHG emissions, sludge generation indicators.

### Scientific Integrity & Rigorous Verification Rules
1. Never fabricate papers, DOIs, parameters, or experimental points. Every value must be categorized (`measured`, `calculated`, `digitized`, `fitted`, `assumed`, or `derived`).
2. Clearly distinguish bCOD from total COD. Do not invent conversion factors.
3. Distinguish HRT ($\tau$) from SRT ($\theta_c$).
4. Physical consistency in PFR biomass retention: do not copy CSTR retention terms without physical justification.
5. All tests must be executed with documented outcomes before claiming completion.
